Subject: Ember Yoga: 3 homepage fixes to turn browse into book

Hi Priya—

Saw you on our Work page just now. You flagged “Traffic but no sales,” so I took a quick pass at Ember’s site.

What’s breaking the booking moment
- “Book a class” jumps to a new Mindbody tab and puts a log‑in/create‑account wall before choosing passes or offers (your /book page explains this, but the jump still surprises people).
- No clear “start here” path splits total beginners from regular yogis; there’s also no cue on which class most people choose first.
- Social proof is thin: two short, generic quotes with no outcomes, locations, or teacher mentions.
- Your intro offer (30 days for £39) is strong, but it isn’t the primary path in the hero.

Three changes you can ship today
1) Make the intro offer the primary path
Use the hero to route new visitors to the offer, and let regulars hit the timetable.

Paste‑ready hero copy
Headline: Find your calm in the city
Sub: New here? 30 days of classes for £39. Start anytime. Valid for new clients only. Excludes workshops and courses. One per person. Activates on first visit.
Buttons: 
- Primary: Start 30 days for £39 → /intro-offer 
- Secondary: See timetable → /book

2) Add a “New here? Start here” chooser under the hero
Two simple cards reduce decision anxiety and stop back‑and‑forth tabbing.

Paste‑ready chooser copy
Left card (for beginners)
- Title: I’m new to yoga
- Body: Start with Beginner or Yin. Go at your pace, focus on form, and get confident on the mat.
- Button: See beginner‑friendly times → /book  
  Dev note: pre‑filter to Beginner and Yin on load.

Right card (for regulars)
- Title: I’ve done yoga before
- Body: Try Hot Yoga 60 or Vinyasa Open. Great everyday flows across Shoreditch, Hackney, Battersea, Camden and Peckham.
- Button: See today’s classes → /book  
  Dev note: pre‑filter to Hot and Vinyasa on load.

3) Strengthen proof where the decision happens
New UK studio best practice this year is to place specific, named, outcome‑focused testimonials right next to the class/timetable/offer—not just in one testimonials strip. Local mentions and teacher names outperform generic praise. Why it matters here: your current quotes are few and generic.

What to collect (and show side‑by‑side with the chooser and near /book)
- Outcome + timeframe: “After three weeks my back pain eased and I started sleeping better.”
- Name + local anchor: “— Maya S., Hackney”
- Optional teacher/class: “Beginner with [Teacher]” or “Hot 60, Shoreditch”

Placement
- One card under each chooser card.
- A narrow proof column on /book beside the timetable with 2–3 local stories.

Reduce the Mindbody drop‑off (one‑week test plan)
- Expectation‑setting step: After clicking “See timetable,” load a light on‑site step that says: “Next you’ll book securely via Mindbody. You’ll be asked to log in or create an account.” Repeat the intro‑offer eligibility here.
- Keep users on‑site longer: If possible, embed the Mindbody timetable/widget on your /book page so the tab change isn’t a surprise.
- Tracking: Add UTMs to hero buttons and note source in the Mindbody checkout notes to see which path starts more purchases (Intro Offer vs. Timetable).

Why this is likely to move the needle
- It removes the first big surprise (new tab + login) and answers the beginner’s “what should I pick?” before they bounce.
- It upgrades proof at the exact moment of doubt with local, teacher‑tied stories instead of generic praise.
