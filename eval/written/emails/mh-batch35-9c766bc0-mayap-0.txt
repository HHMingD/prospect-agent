Maya —

Short version: your two biggest leaks are (1) no live availability/price in the first screen, and (2) a warning about leaving to a portal. Both make people hesitate before they ever see a room.

What to change first (top to bottom)

1) Hero = live booking, not brochure
- Put Arrive, Depart, Guests and a bold “Check availability” in the hero on desktop and mobile.
- Show a price immediately: “From £… for your dates” or, if full, “Next available … from £…”. Seeing a concrete price keeps people moving.
- Under the button, keep your three direct-booking perks in a single sticky row: “10% off · Flexible cancellation · Free parking”. That row should follow the user as they scroll on mobile.
- Keep a secondary link: “See rooms”. Primary action is always availability.

2) Stop warning about the portal; reassure instead
- Replace “You’ll be taken to our secure booking portal…” with a small lock icon + “Secure checkout”. Warnings look like exits.
- If your booking engine must be off-site, delay any redirect until after dates are chosen and a room type is visible. The decision point should happen on your domain.

3) Make prices feel real for the visitor
- On Rooms, swap the static “From £129 BB” labels for dynamic prices for the selected dates. If dates aren’t chosen yet, pull a real “from” for the next 30 days or prompt to pick dates.
- When a date is unavailable, offer the nearest available dates inline with a one-tap option to switch.

4) Hide the promo code until it’s asked for
- Move the promo code into a collapsed “Have a promo code?” link that expands the field. Visible empty code boxes make non‑code guests go hunting (and often leave).

5) Fix the currency symbols (trust leak)
- I’m seeing “Â£” instead of “£” in several places. Ensure the site declares UTF‑8 in the <head> and via the server header, and avoid double‑encoding £ in templates.

6) Mobile-first friction removers
- Add a sticky bottom bar on mobile: “Check availability” that jumps to (or opens) the date picker.
- Keep the phone number tap‑to‑call, but reduce its visual weight vs. the booking action.

Copy you can lift
- Hero header: “Wake up to fell views.”
- Sub: “Book direct for 10% off, flexible cancellation and free parking.”
- CTA: “Check availability”  |  Secondary: “See rooms”
- Trust note near the CTA: “Secure checkout”.

How to tell it’s working (no ad platform needed)
- Track: views of the hero availability module, availability searches started, room selection, checkout start (redirect), and booking complete (if your engine can ping back).
- Success is an increase in “search started → room selected” and “room selected → checkout start”. Baseline these this week, then compare after the hero change.

If you do one thing this week: move a live availability + price block into the first screen and make your perks sticky on mobile. That alone will lift intent without redesigning the site.
