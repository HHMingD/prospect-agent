Hi Maya —

Short, useful, and built for this week while Meta’s new GEM is still in its learning window. Here’s the readout we use for hotels in week one and the next tweaks to ship today.

Your week‑one readout (check daily, then week‑to‑date)
1) Booking‑engine sessions per 100 paid lander visits (PS/100). This is the week‑one truth metric.
- Rising day‑over‑day: keep creative stable; let GEM explore.
- Flat/sub‑30: the lander isn’t matching intent or the hand‑off is slow.

2) Search starts per booking‑engine session (SS/SESS). Are people actually starting an availability check?
- Low = friction in engine start (date/guest prefill, mobile lag).

3) Rate views per search start (RV/SS). Do guests see rooms/prices after their first search?
- Low = availability mapping or price presentation issue.

Everything else (CTR, CPM) is noise in week one. Meta’s own GEM launch cites up to 18.9% more conversions and 12% higher CTR from its unified model; that upside shows up only if your first screen and the hand‑off are tight.

Two tweaks to ship today
- Lander hero: put the date picker + from‑price beside one sticky “Check availability,” and state “Book direct: 10% off, flexible cancellation, free parking.” Add a compact review badge in the first screen. Trim the header to essentials.
- Handoff hygiene: carry UTMs into the booking portal; prefill check‑in/out and guests; keep UTMs and selections if a user returns. If you see a big drop from CTA click to engine session, the external jump is likely the constraint. Hotels moving to fully integrated, embedded engines have recently doubled direct desktop conversion and nearly tripled mobile conversion versus older platforms; the closer the flow feels to “one site,” the better this paid test will perform.
