Milo —

You asked for pointed answers you can use on a real drop. Here’s the short version, framed for a January Sundrift drop and pitched to a skeptic.

What to cut from the drop page (so paid traffic converts)
- Global nav and catalog: remove it. Keep only logo (home), Help, T&Cs in the footer. One page, one job.
- Secondary CTAs: no newsletter, no “learn more,” no social icons. One primary CTA: Add to Cart on the starter bundle.
- Entry popups: none. Exit‑intent only, delayed ≥25s, single field.
- App bloat: spin wheels, chat bubbles, quiz modals, press carousels. Kill them for the drop.
- Heavy hero: no autoplay video. Lazy‑load any motion; keep LCP clean.
- Cross‑sells/UGC walls: move below the fold or to post‑purchase.
- Redundant detail: collapse Ingredients/Nutrition/Shipping into accordions; default closed.

What to refresh first on creative (drop week)
- Hero variant 1 — Social‑proof‑first: hero still + 1 short testimonial (real name + avatar) above the fold; headline framework: “Limited run. [Why it’s better] in every [sip/serve].”
- Hero variant 2 — Scarcity‑first: hero still + countdown to ship date or inventory cap; headline framework: “Limited to [N] batches. Ships [date window].”
- Hero variant 3 — In‑use‑first: tight pour/hand shot; three bullets: What it is, Why it’s better, Delivered price. CTA: “Get the Starter Bundle.”
- Ad cadence: always have fresh in queue. Retire any ad that sits below your baseline CTR for 3 weeks. Keep three angles live: scarcity, social proof, and in‑use/benefit. 

Why I’m this opinionated: two relevant moves
- Oatside Kitchen cut cost per first order 38% by moving ads off the homepage onto three offer pages. Focus wins.
- Sneak Energy’s D&D drop works because everything points to one collab page with a limited‑run hero, reviews above the fold, and a sticky ATC. Anchor a pre‑selected starter bundle to free shipping and show delivered price.

Wireframe (one‑page drop, paste‑ready frameworks)
1) Thin bar: “Free shipping over £X. Ships [date window].” (Set £X to just below your target AOV.)
2) Hero: 
   - H1: “Sundrift [Drop Name]. Limited run.”
   - Sub: 1 sentence “Cleaner [benefit], zero [objection], tastes like [flavor cue].”
   - Price stack: “£[price] — £[delivered price] delivered” (auto‑updates when threshold hit)
   - Primary CTA: “Get the Starter Bundle” (pre‑selected)
   - Micro‑proof row: 3 avatars + short quotes (or rating stars if you have them)
3) Bundle module (above the fold):
   - Starter bundle pre‑selected; single‑unit as secondary option in a toggle
   - Quantity stepper; “Ships [date window]”
4) Benefits row (3 icons): What it is / Why it’s better / How it tastes
5) Reviews snapshot (still above the fold on desktop; first scroll on mobile): 3–5 shorties with names
6) Details accordions: Ingredients; Nutrition; How to make/enjoy; Shipping & returns
7) FAQ (3 Qs): “When does it ship?” “How many servings?” “Can I subscribe later?”
8) Minimal footer: Help, T&Cs
9) Sticky ATC: product name + price + Add to Cart always visible

Week‑one tests (drop‑first)
- T1: Hero angle. Social‑proof‑first vs Scarcity‑first vs In‑use‑first. Success = higher ATC rate + no CVR trade‑off.
- T2: Proof placement. Reviews snapshot above the fold vs first scroll. Success = +ATC and lower bounce.
- T3: Price framing. Show “£[price] delivered” vs “£[price] + shipping.” Success = higher checkout start rate.
- T4: Default selection. Starter bundle pre‑selected vs single unit pre‑selected. Success = higher AOV without CVR loss.
- T5: Threshold callout. Free‑shipping line in thin bar only vs thin bar + inline under CTA. Success = higher AOV.

- Add‑to‑Cart rate from LP sessions: 12–18% on warm, 6–12% on cold.
- Purchase conversion from LP sessions: 3–5% on warm, 1.5–3% on cold.
- AOV lift from threshold: +12–20% when the starter bundle is the default and the threshold is visible.
- Page speed: keep LCP under ~2.5s; image‑first hero wins consistency.
